# PREREG 112 -- Coma broken down to its quantum constituents: does everything add up to the pull? (Coalesce's idea)
Committed BEFORE running iter112_inventory.py.
Target: Coma's mass from its galaxy speeds / lensing, 1.6e15 Msun (project value, iterations 94/98); visible 3e14
(hot gas 2.5e14 + stars 0.5e14; Hughes 1989, galaxiesbook.org). Volume: sphere R = 2.5 Mpc (sensitivity 2 and 3 Mpc).
Inventory -- every KNOWN particle/field, nothing invented; all energy counted as mass (E = mc^2), sign kept:
 1 protons, neutrons, electrons in gas + stars (X = 0.75, Y = 0.25 by mass) -- counted particle by particle, then the nuclear
   binding of helium (-0.7%) and the quark-level split of the proton (quarks ~1%, rest is gluon-field energy: already inside)
 2 heat of the gas (kT = 8.2 keV)         3 gravitational binding of the visible matter (negative)
 4 starlight inside the cluster now + all starlight ever made there (upper bound)
 5 relic photons (CMB) inside the volume   6 magnetic field (1-5 microgauss)   7 cosmic rays (<= few % of heat; upper 10%)
 8 relic neutrinos: cosmic mean + the most a cluster can trap (Pauli/phase-space limit, sigma 1000 km/s), masses from
   oscillations (0, 0.0086, 0.050 eV) and an extreme case 3 x 0.1 eV (already excluded by DESI)
 9 central black holes (<= 0.2% of star mass)   10 measured vacuum energy (dark energy) in the volume, gravity of rho+3p
11 naive quantum vacuum energy (Planck cutoff) -- shown only to see whether it can be what gravitates
Free choices: cluster (Coma), radius, component bounds (taken at the generous end). Look-elsewhere: one cluster, one ratio.
Expectations: all known constituents sum to 0.18-0.20 of the needed mass; everything beyond protons/neutrons/electrons adds
< 1e-3 of the needed mass; neutrinos < 1e-4. Naive vacuum energy: ~1e120 times too much, and repulsive -> cannot be it.

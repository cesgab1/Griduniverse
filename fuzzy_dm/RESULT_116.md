# RESULT 116 -- fuzzy (wave) dark matter vs 34 Milky Way dwarfs
Data: Simon 2019 Table 1, all 34 dwarfs with measured sigma; read in browser, column sums verified (R 9397, sigma 207.8);
Fornax sigma 11.7 matches the project's verified value.
Minimum particle mass per dwarf (dark core no more compact than one quantum wave): strongest Grus I 2.0e-20, Segue 1 1.9e-20,
Leo V 1.5e-20 eV (central); conservative (sigma and R at +1 error): Segue 1 1.2e-20, Hydrus I 9.0e-21, Willman 1 8.4e-21.
Population: 33/34 dwarfs need > 1e-22, 22 need > 1e-21, 16 > 2e-21, 5 > 1e-20 (1 conservatively), none > 1e-19.
sigma x r1/2 varies x315 across dwarfs -> they cannot all be single solitons of one particle mass.
Fornax-as-soliton mass 1.8e-22 eV is excluded x65-100 by the smallest dwarfs.
Against expectations: strongest ~1e-20 HIT; several > 2e-21 HIT (16); spread > x30 HIT (x315); Fornax mass excluded >x30 HIT.
Verdict: a galaxy-sized quantum wave is NOT ruled out, but it must be at least ~1e-20 eV, which shrinks the wave below
~30 pc -- too small to shape the cores of classical dwarfs like Fornax (its main original attraction). Agrees in direction
with published limits (Lyman-alpha ~2e-21, star heating ~3e-19 eV; memory). Note: binary stars inflate sigma, and the bound
scales as 1/sigma, so binaries make these bounds conservative.
Caveats: assumes equilibrium and dark-dominated dwarfs; tidal stripping (e.g. Crater II, Sagittarius) loosens their entries;
(the 'strongest three' line in the .txt lists the top by central value with conservative numbers -- see above for both.)

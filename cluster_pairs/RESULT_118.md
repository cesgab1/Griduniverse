# RESULT 118 -- same-size clusters and groups vs 'visible matter alone sets gravity'
31 systems (10 clusters Vikhlinin+2006, 21 groups Sun+2009 after duplicates); boost B = total/visible = x6.5-11.5 (stars
included), median x8.9.
T1 acceleration scale: best a = 2.7e-10 (stars) to 3.5e-10 (no stars) m/s^2 = x2-3 the galaxies' 1.2e-10; with galaxies'
   value the rule falls short by x1.4-1.6 (same as Coma, iteration 113). Expectation x5-20 -- MISS: clusters are closer
   to the galaxy rule than I expected (my memory of 'a ~ 1e-9' for clusters came from studies at smaller radii).
T2 same size, different boost: intrinsic scatter 0.04-0.05 dex (x1.10), 4.9 sigma in the combined sample -- BUT post-hoc
   check: within one team's sample only 2.3 sigma, and ~30% group-to-group variation in star mass would explain it.
   -> NOT decisive. (Expectation 0.05-0.12 dex: roughly met; significance claim withdrawn.)
T3 trend with size: the rule needs the visible fraction to rise ~in proportion to size (slope 0.7-1); measured slope
   0.12 / 0.35 / 0.44 for the three star assumptions -> 8.0 / 4.4 / 2.6 sigma below 0.7 (bootstrap). HIT in direction;
   strength depends on how star fractions scale with mass (the main free choice).
Verdict: a single 'boost set by visible pull' rule cannot fit groups and clusters together -- it needs a larger
acceleration scale than galaxies (x2-3) AND a much steeper rise of visible fraction with size than observed. The cleanest
evidence is T3; T2 is inconclusive. Caveats: hydrostatic masses ~10-20% low; two samples with h 0.72/0.73; stellar
fractions from a memory-based relation (free choice, three versions reported).

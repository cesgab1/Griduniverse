# PREREG 121 -- 'everpresent Lambda': dark energy as a counting fluctuation of grid points (Sorkin) vs data
Committed BEFORE running iter121_everpresent.py.
Model (simplest form, after Sorkin; Ahmed, Dodelson, Greene & Sorkin 2004 -- memory): space-time = randomly scattered
points, ~1 per Planck 4-volume. Lambda = (random sum over all past points) / (past 4-volume): Lambda(V) = W(V)/V, W a
random walk with variance V. So Lambda jitters, typically ~1/sqrt(V), with random sign; new points keep adding.
Approximation: past 4-volume V ~ t^4 (cosmic time t from the standard expansion history); ignores the jitter's own effect
on expansion. The amplitude alpha cancels: every test below uses RATIOS of dark-energy density at different times.
Data criteria (generous, chosen to cover both supernova catalogues' free-bin fits, iterations D3/S1):
 (a) dark energy positive today;
 (b) dark-energy density at z = 0.4, 0.8, 1.5 between 0.5x and 2x today's;
 (c) early universe: dark energy below 5% of matter at z = 1100 (CMB 'early dark energy' limits ~ few %, memory).
Statistic: fraction of 200000 random realisations meeting (a)-(c); constant Lambda meets all with probability 1.
Expectation: (b) alone met in ~5-15% of realisations (dark energy should have been ~10x larger at z ~ 1.5); (c) met in
<1%; all together < 0.5% -> the simplest everpresent Lambda is disfavoured; a version needs extra damping of past
fluctuations (a free choice) to survive. Free choices: V ~ t^4, envelope widths (stated).

# Pre-registration, iteration 91: is light fading on its way to us (extra dimming), and could it explain the Hubble tension?
(Oct 5 2026, Coalesce's idea; written BEFORE running)
Physics: distances measured by BRIGHTNESS (supernovae, luminosity distance) and by SIZE (BAO / CMB, angular distance) must obey
d_L = (1+z)^2 d_A if photons are not lost on the way (Etherington). Extra fading = supernovae look farther than BAO says.
Test: supernova distances multiplied by (1+z)^eps (eps > 0 = extra fading), fitted with BAO + CMB priors, constant dark energy and
Claim 1; 3 supernova sets. Also compare the fitted H0 with/without eps against the local ladder 73.0 +/- 1.0.
Expectations: eps consistent with 0 within ~0.02-0.05 (CMB is a perfect black body, so photon loss is limited); H0 essentially
unchanged (~67-68), because the supernova absolute brightness is free and the H0 comes from BAO + CMB -> fading cannot fix the
tension. Note: fading would make distant objects look FARTHER, i.e. mimic MORE acceleration, not less.

# PREREG 125: does reading sharpness depend on capacity only through h/m?
Committed before collecting data and before code.

Idea (Coalesce): lower-capacity particles read the plane differently. Quantum theory says a particle's footprint is
exactly h/(m v): capacity enters only through h/m. A 'capacity correction' would show up as h/m measured with atoms
of different mass disagreeing with each other.
Cleanest data: the fine-structure constant alpha measured three ways:
  (i) h/m of caesium-133 atoms, (ii) h/m of rubidium-87 atoms, (iii) the electron's magnetic moment (g-2; no h/m).
alpha^2 is proportional to h/m_atom, so a capacity correction eps(m) on h/m shifts alpha by eps/2.
Known in advance (stated honestly): Cs and Rb values are reported to disagree by ~5 sigma.
Test: model eps(m) = A (m / m_Rb)^p with ONE sign (a monotonic capacity effect); taking (iii) as the
capacity-free reference, both atoms' deviations must have the SAME sign. Opposite signs, each > 2 sigma, excludes any
single monotonic capacity law (points to experimental systematics). Also judged by project rules: significance,
independent confirmation, strengthening with data, population (only 2 atoms -> cannot be a population).
Expectation (written now): deviations have opposite signs -> monotonic capacity law excluded; tension most likely
experimental. Secondary (if found): neutron h/m, Kibble-balance vs atom-counting kilogram agreement.
Free choices: which g-2 value as reference (latest), 1.

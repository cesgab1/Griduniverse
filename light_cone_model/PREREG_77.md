# Pre-registration, iteration 77: are neutrinos dark energy's clock? (Oct 4 2026, written BEFORE running)
Measured inputs only: NuFIT 6.0 (2024) mass splittings dm21^2 = 7.49e-5 eV^2, |dm31^2| = 2.513e-3 eV^2; relic neutrino temperature
1.945 K; DESI DR2 + CMB bound sum(m) < 0.0642 eV (95%). Our derived 2.24 meV is circular and NOT used.
'Slows down' (turns non-relativistic) when the mean momentum 3.15 T_nu(z) equals m c (criterion 1) or sqrt(3) m c (kinetic energy =
rest energy, criterion 2): one convention choice, both reported.
N1 Clock: when do the neutrinos with the two MEASURED mass scales (>= 8.65 meV and >= 50.1 meV) slow down, vs acceleration onset
   (z = 0.67, 7.5 Gyr)? Expectation: far too early (z ~ 15 and ~ 90, well under 1 Gyr) -> MISS.
N2 Only the unmeasured lightest neutrino could slow down 'now'. Required m1 for onset / crossing (z = 0.67 / 0.32).
   Expectation ~0.6 - 1.5 meV. NOTE: this is a FIT (one free number in, one out), not a prediction.
   Consequences that ARE testable: normal ordering required (inverted gives sum >= ~99 meV vs bound 64 -> excluded);
   sum ~ 59.5 - 60 meV, inside today's bound but indistinguishable from m1 = 0 with today's precision.
N3 Energy scale: dark energy's 2.24 meV vs the measured scales 8.65 and 50.1 meV; PASS if within x2. Expectation: FAIL (x3.9, x22).
N4 Energy budget: neutrino energy today vs dark energy. Expectation: neutrinos ~0.14% of the total, ~500 x smaller than dark
   energy -> neutrinos cannot BE dark energy; only a coupling could link them (known clumping-instability problem in the
   literature for mass-varying-neutrino models: Afshordi, Zaldarriaga & Kohri 2005).

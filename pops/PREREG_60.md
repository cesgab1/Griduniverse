# Pre-registration, iteration 60 (committed BEFORE computing): WHY only electrons hold a pop open
Iteration 58 needed the rule 'protons do not pay'. Candidate reason, fixed now:
 R1 A particle can hold a pop open at its own pop size (hbar/mc) only if the particle itself is SMALLER than that pop size
    (otherwise its charge is spread over more than the pair separation and there is no 'open pop' to hold).
    Point-like charged particles pass; composite ones larger than their own pop size fail.
 Expected: electron passes (size < 1e-18 m vs pop 3.9e-13 m); proton fails (0.84 fm vs 0.21 fm); helium-4 nucleus fails
    (1.68 fm vs 0.053 fm). Only electrons pay -> the proton catastrophe of iteration 58 is avoided without a hand-made rule.
 Consistency with payday: at z = 124 almost all electrons are bound in atoms (free fraction ~2e-4). The handover is set by the
    COLLECTIVE thermal tie of the whole gas to the light (kept by the few free electrons), so all electrons, bound or free, pay
    at the same moment -- this matches the 'global' requirement from white dwarfs and reionisation. Expect: consistent.
 New consequence to state: neutral particles (neutrinos, dark matter) hold no Coulomb pop -> never pay.

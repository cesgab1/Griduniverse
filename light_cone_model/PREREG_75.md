# Pre-registration, iteration 75: can 'starts big at the Bang, fades fast' survive the early record? (Oct 4 2026, BEFORE running)
Data limits used (real): extra energy <= 4% of the total at the first 3 minutes (N_eff = 2.99 +/- 0.17 and helium);
early dark energy <= 0.4% around transparency (Planck 2018, persistent early dark energy, Omega_e < 0.0036).
Histories: dark energy starts at a natural share of 1/2 at the Planck time, fades as a^(-n), then FREEZES when it reaches the
late-time value and continues as (A) a constant at today's value, or (B) the fading law (value from iteration 74 at that moment).
Natural fade laws (no fitting): n = 3 (like matter), 4 (like radiation), 6 ('kination': a field's pure motion energy, stiffest
possible). Freeze time a_s then follows with no freedom. Check: early limits, and whether a_s lands within x3 in temperature of a
named event (Planck, electroweak 160 GeV, QCD 0.15 GeV, e+e- annihilation 0.5 MeV, first 3 minutes 0.07 MeV, equality, transparency).
Comparisons: 3 laws x 2 endpoints x 7 events = 42.
DISCLOSURE: while setting this up, a back-of-envelope estimate suggested n = 6 freezes near the QCD era (~1e-12 in a). Because I saw
that before pre-registering, a QCD hit for n = 6 counts for NOTHING by itself; look-elsewhere chance is high (7 events spread over
~22 decades in temperature, x3 windows -> roughly 1 in 3 per viable law by chance).
Expectations: n = 3 impossible (never freezes before today; grows vs radiation -> fails the 3-minute limit). n = 4 impossible
(share stays 1/2 until it freezes -> fails 3-minute limit, or can't reach today's value). n = 6: passes both limits; freezes
around a ~ 1e-12 for endpoint A; endpoint B different by ~x(1e5)^(1/6) in a.
Main conclusion expected: the early record does NOT exclude 'starts big, fades fast'; it only moves the dial to 'when it froze'.

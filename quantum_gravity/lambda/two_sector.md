# Two sectors: frame-free gravity, one frame-dependent field (Oct 2026)
Re-derivation of the small-scale results after adopting 'time is its own (random) dimension'.

## What carries over (BORROWED)
- Grids random in spacetime (causal sets) with averaged (nonlocal) wave operators show a universal reduction to 2 dimensions at
  small scales, in any dimension (Belenchia, Benincasa, Marciano & Modesto 2016, arXiv:1507.00330). So 'looks different at the
  quantum level' (E14, iteration 42) survives for the frame-free grid.
- BUT turning 2 small-scale dimensions into a scale-invariant 'watermark' needs size-dependent wave speeds, i.e. a preferred frame
  (Amelino-Camelia, Arzano, Gubitosi & Magueijo 2013, arXiv:1305.3153). A frame-free grid alone cannot print the watermark.

## The structure that keeps both
- Sector 1 (gravity): the N layers that induce gravity live on the spacetime-random grid -> frame-free -> lambda = 1 by symmetry.
- Sector 2 (one field): the tension/slicing field (carries Claim 1's dark energy and the z = 3 small-scale rule) is the only
  frame-dependent piece. It prints the watermark (Mukohyama mechanism, iteration 30: needs a spectator field with z = 3, not
  gravity itself) and sets the 'now' (constant-expansion slices).
- Its leakage into gravity is diluted by the number of layers: deviations ~ c/N with c an O(1) number (rough; not computed).

## Consequences (two_sector_numbers.txt)

| lambda - 1 (G_cosmo/G_N) | 0.017 | N >~ 60 |

The allowed window was N = 2.9e7 (smoothness) to 5.2e15 (gamma-ray bursts). With c = 1 the graviton-speed bound pushes N to the
TOP of the window: N ~ 1e15 - 5e15, cell size 2.5e-28 - 5.7e-28 m, right at today's gamma-ray-burst limit -> near-testable by
better burst timing. With c = 0.1 (0.01): N >~ 1e14 (1e13), cell >~ 8e-29 (2.5e-29) m.

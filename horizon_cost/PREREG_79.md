# Pre-registration, iteration 79: what does a horizon 'cost', and what sets the horizon? (Oct 4 2026, written BEFORE running)
Step 1 (circularity check). Three horizon-cost rules, each with two horizons (event horizon of the fading history 6.24 Gpc; Hubble
radius c/H0 = 4.45 Gpc) = 6 numbers:
  H1 black-hole cost (energy inside = black hole of that size): rho = 3 c^2 / (8 pi G R^2)
  H2 our quantum rule with the far-future smallest length l = 1.64 l_P: rho = hbar c / (2 l^2 R^2)
  H3 horizon glow (Gibbons-Hawking temperature, black-body energy): rho = (pi^2/15) (k T)^4/(hbar c)^3, T = hbar c/(2 pi k R)
Expectation: H1, H2 within x0.5-2 of measured; H3 ~1e-120 x measured. AND: H1/H2 'work' only because Einstein's equation already
says total density x (Hubble radius)^2 = 3 c^2/(8 pi G) for ANY universe -- the horizon is set by the contents, so this is circular.
Step 2 (break the loop: set the horizon by an outside clock). R = c x (lifetime of stars), the epoch when observers exist.
  Star clock from particle constants alone (Carr & Rees 1979 estimate): t_star ~ alpha^2 (m_p/m_e)^2 (m_Planck/m_p)^2 hbar/(m_p c^2),
  O(1) factors unknown. Also the measured Sun main-sequence lifetime (~10 Gyr) -- choosing the Sun is a free (observer) choice.
  Prediction rho = 3/(8 pi G t^2) (H1 with R = c t).
Expectation: particle-constant clock ~0.5-1 Gyr -> rho ~50-300 x measured (vs the naive 1e120 mismatch); Sun's 10 Gyr -> ~1-3 x.
Counting: 2 clocks; this is a Dicke-type (observer) argument, not a mechanism. It reduces the 1e120 problem to the O(1) factors
in stellar physics; it does not explain why dark energy is constant.

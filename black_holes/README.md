# Black holes: Planck stars, leaking, end state, supermassive black holes

## Planck stars (loop quantum gravity; Rovelli & Vidotto 2014, Haggard & Rovelli 2015)
Collapse stops at Planck *density*, not Planck size: the core is ~ (M/m_P)^(1/3) Planck lengths across (1e-22 m for a stellar hole,
1e-19 m for M87*), far larger than a Planck length. Quantum pressure makes it bounce into a white hole. Seen from outside, time
dilation stretches the bounce enormously, so it looks like an ordinary black hole for a very long time.

Grid reading: links cannot be squeezed past a minimum size, so a collapsing knot "bottoms out" and springs back. This is our
"no singularity, it leaks" intuition. In Khronon theory the bounce happens on the grid's own layers (the preferred slicing).

## What happens in the end, and where does the mass go?
- tau ~ M^2 scenario: the whole mass comes back out in one burst (radiation and particles) into our own universe. Nothing goes elsewhere.
- tau ~ M^3 scenario (where the field has moved, 2020-2024 papers): almost all mass leaves first as Hawking radiation; a Planck-mass
  (~micrograms) white-hole remnant is left, leaking its information out over ~M^4-M^5 times.
- Does it become "fluid"? Not by any known process. The released energy is ordinary radiation and particles. Planck-mass remnants
  would be cold and dust-like (gravitationally like the dark fluid), but astrophysical holes cannot have made any yet. Only
  primordial holes could, and that is a separate, untested dark-matter idea (Rovelli & Vidotto 2018).
- Objections: white-hole instability (Barcelo, Carballo-Rubio & Garay: "turn white fast, otherwise stay black"); mass inflation.
  Replies exist; unresolved. No signal (FRB/gamma-ray burst from exploding primordial holes) has been seen.

## Supermassive black holes
Our model does not yet explain them. Planck stars say nothing about their origin (no paper links them). The JWST puzzle is that
1e6-1e8 Msun holes already exist at z ~ 10 ("overmassive" holes, little red dots), favouring heavy seeds.
Hypothesis to test (not derived): the dark fluid is self-interacting (our channel gas is a strongly repelling Tonks gas;
the superfluid-DM fits used sigma/m ~ 0.01 cm^2/g). If its interactions can shed energy, cold early halo cores undergo
gravothermal collapse straight into heavy seeds of ~0.3% of the halo mass (dissipative self-interacting DM seeding, arXiv:2504.00075).
Test: does the fluid's interaction dissipate energy, and does the seed mass function match JWST counts at z ~ 6-10?

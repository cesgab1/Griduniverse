# PREREG 131: where IC 348's dust came from and why it collects there
Committed before data and code. Uses lessons of 129-130 (addendum allowed before running).
(a) SOURCES: published dust production rates in the Milky Way (dying Sun-like stars = AGB winds; supernovae) vs
    destruction time (supernova shocks). Compute: dust made by stars over one destruction time vs dust observed.
    Expectation: stars alone fall short by a factor of several -> most dust re-grows inside cold clouds (a measured
    'budget gap'); SN 1987A dust (ALMA) as a direct check that supernovae make dust.
(b) GATHERING: Perseus (with IC 348) lies on a supernova-blown shell (Per-Tau shell, 3-D dust maps). Classical
    snowplough: mass swept by a sphere of the published radius through the measured average gas density, times the
    dust fraction (~1%). Expectation: swept mass ~1e5 Msun, comparable to the combined Perseus + Taurus cloud mass.
(c) INSIDE DISKS: classical drag physics -> how fast mm grains drift inward in a star's disk vs a brown dwarf's disk.
    Expectation: drift FASTER (relative to disk size) around brown dwarfs, so mm grains should vanish within
    ~1 Myr unless trapped; mm grains ARE seen in brown-dwarf disks at Myr ages -> traps (pressure bumps) required.
Free choices: ambient density, shell radius, disk model (stated).

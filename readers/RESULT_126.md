# RESULT 126: anti-readers -- same capacity, opposite interpretation?
Prereg: PREREG_126.md. Code: iter126_anti_readers.py. Numbers: RESULT_126_numbers.txt.
(a) Capacity: particle and antiparticle masses/ratios equal to 6e-19 (neutral kaons), 1.6e-11 (proton q/m),
    2e-12 (electron g), 8e-9 (electron mass). Expectation (equal to 1e-11..1e-18): HIT.
(b) Asymmetry vs mass across K0, D0, B0, Bs, Lambda_b: Spearman rho = +0.50 (p = 0.39) headline; +0.70 (p = 0.19)
    alternative (direct asymmetries). No significant trend. Expectation: HIT -- but LOW POWER (5 systems, three at
    5.3-5.6 GeV), so this cannot rule a mass trend out either. The mild rise in the alternative is what the Standard
    Model already expects (heavier hadrons have more interfering decay routes), not a capacity effect.
(c) Budget: the measured particle/antiparticle difference, in the standard measure at 100 GeV, is 1.2e-19
    (literature: ~1e-20); the matter excess needs 6.1e-10 -> short by ~5e9. Expectation (~1e10): HIT.
    This is a measured gap: the known anti-reader differences are far too small to leave today's matter.

LESSON 126: anti-readers have the SAME capacity (to 1e-18) and read most things identically; they differ only in
how they interpret a few rare processes (weak-force phases). And that measured difference is ~5 billion times too
small to explain why matter won -- a real gap, the same kind as the missing-pull gap: observed, not filled.

# Can the dark fluid change state / stiffness so it stays out of galaxies but clumps elsewhere? (Coalesce's proposal, Oct 2026)

1. Rate-dependent stiffness (viscoelastic / Maxwell fluid, relaxation time tau): viscoelastic_window.*
   Collapse time depends only on formation epoch (t_ff = 0.111/H(z_f)). Extended Press-Schechter formation times:
   galaxies 1e11-1e12 Msun z_f ~ 0.9-1.1 (t_ff 0.84-0.94 Gyr), clusters 1e14-1e15 z_f ~ 0.4-0.55 (1.17-1.30 Gyr): only 1.5x apart.
   Best tau (1.2 Gyr): 15-25% of galaxies still capture halos (SPARC's tight relation forbids this), only 46-71% of clusters do. FAILS.

2. Any single trigger (trigger_axes.*): rate, speed, stress (density x speed^2), mass scale, potential depth.
   The fluid must clump in CMB-era perturbations, Lyman-alpha structures (z~3), today's large-scale structure and clusters, but not in
   galaxies. Galaxies are never at an extreme of any of these: on every axis some 'must-clump' case lies below them and another above.
   A single threshold (stiff above X, or soft below X) cannot do it; it would need a two-sided band or a combination of quantities.
   (Order-of-magnitude values; the ordering, not the exact numbers, is the result.)

Lesson: galaxies sit in the middle of every simple axis. Rather than keeping the fluid OUT of galaxies, the remaining clean route is that
the fluid IN galaxies is what produces the MOND effect (the halo and the MOND pull are one thing, not added together), the original
'ocean' picture. Berezhiani-Khoury has that structure but fails lensing because its push bypasses the geometry; making the push act
through the grid (Khronon) is the untested combination.

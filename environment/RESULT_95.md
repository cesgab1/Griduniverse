# RESULT 95 -- does a cluster environment change how galaxies behave?
Outputs: iter95_environment.txt, iter95_posthoc.txt (post-hoc, labelled).

A. Virgo (nearest cluster): kappa = 0.10-0.63 vs the Fornax dwarf's 0.039 -> x2.7-16 (Coma: x5). Virgo shows the same
   cluster gap. Range dominated by which baryon mass/aperture is used (disclosed). MET.
   Virgo's pull at the Milky Way: 3.6e-13 m/s^2 = 0.003 a0: far too weak to affect our galaxy. MET (< 0.01 a0).
B. Galaxies in the Ursa Major cluster (27) vs others (123), residual from v^4 = G M_b a0:
   pre-registered mean comparison: +0.106 +/- 0.047 dex (+2.2 sigma; members ~6% faster at fixed mass) -> MISSED the "within
   2 sigma" expectation, narrowly. Post-hoc: driven by ~5 field outliers (field scatter 0.41 dex vs 0.15 for members);
   medians differ by 0.067 dex; 3-sigma-clipped +0.051 +/- 0.041 (1.2 sigma). Sign is opposite to MOND's external-field
   effect (which would make cluster members slightly SLOWER). Most likely cause: field galaxies' distances are less certain
   (Hubble flow) while members share one good cluster distance. Verdict: no environment effect established.
C. Milky Way: law predicts 168-183 km/s for M_b 5-7e10 vs ~200 observed at 25 kpc: 8-16% low; at the edge of the 15%
   expectation (MET at 6-7e10, MISSED at 5e10). Crude: one number for a galaxy whose curve still declines.
Overall: a galaxy behaves the same whether in the field or in a loose cluster; clusters themselves (Virgo, Coma) carry ~3-16x
more pull than their visible matter supplies under the galaxy law.
Sources: Kashibadze et al. 2020 A&A 635 A135 (Virgo sigma, masses, 16.5 Mpc); Simionescu et al. 2017 MNRAS 469 1476 (r200,
M200, gas fraction); Eilers et al. 2019 (Milky Way curve); SPARC (Lelli et al. 2016), Ursa Major members at 18.0 Mpc.

# RESULT 131: where IC 348's dust came from and why it collects there
Prereg: PREREG_131.md. Code: iter131_dust.py. Numbers: RESULT_131_numbers.txt.
(a) SOURCES. Dying Sun-like and giant stars (winds, planetary nebulae) inject ~0.005 Msun/yr of dust; supernova
    shocks destroy dust in 0.35-0.4 Gyr (Draine 2009; Zhukovska 2016) or 2-3 Gyr for silicates (Slavin 2015).
    Stardust standing stock = 7-8% of the observed ~2.5e7 Msun (short lifetimes) or 50% (long) -> 50-93% of the dust
    must have RE-GROWN inside cold clouds (atoms sticking onto surviving grains). Expectation (stars short by a
    factor of several): HIT (factor 2-13, depending on the destruction-time convention).
    Supernovae make dust (SN 1987A: 0.5-0.8 Msun measured); if most survived its own reverse shock, supernovae
    alone would double the stellar input (rate ~2 per century, from memory).
(b) GATHERING. Perseus (with IC 348, 321 pc) sits on the Per-Tau shell, a 156-pc bubble blown by multiple
    supernovae 6-22 Myr ago (Bialy 2021). Snowplough: the bubble swept 0.7-3.4e5 Msun of gas (ambient density 1-5
    per cm^3, not published -> range) carrying ~350-3400 Msun of dust; Perseus + Taurus clouds (3.4e4 Msun) = 10-50%
    of it. Expectation (~1e5, comparable): HIT.
(c) INSIDE DISKS. Drag drift of 1 mm grains from half the disk radius: Sun-like star ~3e4 yr; brown dwarf ~7e3 yr
    (4x faster); a 2-Jupiter-mass object ~240 yr. mm grains are SEEN in brown-dwarf disks at 1-3 Myr -> drift must be
    stopped by traps (pressure bumps) by factors of >~100 (brown dwarf) to >~1e4 (2 MJup). Expectation (faster
    around brown dwarfs; traps needed): HIT (more extreme than the ~1 Myr expected).

LESSON 131: the dust in IC 348 is recycled material: made by earlier generations of stars across the Galaxy
(dying stars' winds, supernovae), mostly re-grown in cold clouds, and swept into Perseus by a supernova-blown bubble
that also triggered the collapse. Inside each tiny disk, dust only survives long enough to build planets if
something traps it -- the smaller the central object, the stronger the trap must be.

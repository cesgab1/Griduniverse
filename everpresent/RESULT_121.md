# RESULT 121 -- simplest 'everpresent Lambda' (dark energy as a counting fluctuation) vs data
200000 random realisations of Lambda = W(V)/V with V ~ t^4. Typical dark-energy density relative to today:
x2.2 at z = 0.4, x4.1 at z = 0.8, x10 at z = 1.5, x1.4e9 at z = 1100 (random sign).
Data criteria met: positive today 50%; within 0.5-2x of today at z = 0.4/0.8/1.5: 0.45%; below 5% of matter at
recombination: 1.4%; all together 0.007% (~4 sigma) -- constant Lambda meets all.
Against expectations: (b) 5-15% -- MISS (0.45%, worse); (c) < 1% -- MISS (1.4%, slightly better); all < 0.5% -- HIT.
Verdict: in its simplest form, dark energy as a fluctuation of the number of grid points is strongly disfavoured: it
should have been ~10x stronger at z ~ 1.5 and ~1e9x at the CMB era, while data show a nearly constant value. To survive,
past fluctuations must be damped by >~ x50 at recombination and kept within x2 since z ~ 1.5 -- a free choice the
simple model does not supply. Caveat: published versions (Ahmed+2004; Zwane, Afshordi & Sorkin 2018 -- memory) include
the jitter's own effect on expansion and other prescriptions and report some viable realisations; this tests only the
basic 1/sqrt(4-volume) scaling. Order-of-magnitude success (Lambda ~ 1/sqrt(N) today) stands; the time behaviour fails.

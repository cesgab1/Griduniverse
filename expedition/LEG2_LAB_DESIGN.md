# Expedition leg 2 -- lab experiment design and predictions on record (Oct 2026)
Question: does the galaxy gap (Layer 2 of the working model) also appear between small lab masses whose OWN pull is
weaker than ~1e-10 m/s^2? Two possibilities, both using only the measured galaxy gap curve (no new numbers):
- H_total: the gap depends on the total pull at a place; Earth's 9.8 m/s^2 switches it off -> lab sees pure Newton.
- H_self:  the gap depends on the source's own pull -> lab sees Newton x the galaxy gap curve.
Design: same torsion-balance technique as Westphal+2021 (90 mg gold; demonstrated 4e-12 m/s^2 statistical, 4e-11
systematic). Measure at a near separation (own pull ~1e-9, gap x1) and a far one; take the RATIO so G and calibration cancel.
| setup | far own pull | H_self far signal | far/near ratio vs Newton |
| 90 mg, 2.4 vs 10 mm | 6.0e-11 | x1.95 | x1.90 |
| 90 mg, 2.4 vs 20 mm | 1.5e-11 | x3.35 | x3.26 |
| 1 g, 5 vs 50 mm     | 2.7e-11 | x2.69 | x2.37 |
H_self's extra signal is 9-14x the demonstrated statistical precision but only ~1x the systematic level -> needs
systematics improved ~x3-5 (the ratio method should remove part of them). H_total predicts x1.00 in every row.
Quantum extension: gravity-entanglement experiments (1e-14 kg, 0.2 mm, own pull ~2e-17) -> H_self: entanglement ~2600x
faster (extrapolated beyond the measured curve); H_total: x1.
Outcome map: ratio = Newton -> the gap is not a property of weak gravity between any two masses (H_self dead; H_total or
unseen mass remain). Ratio = H_self row -> the gap IS weak gravity itself, and the cluster/Bullet results then demand
something more. Either result moves the working model from 'measured gap' toward 'law'.

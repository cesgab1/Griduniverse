# Iteration 66: which tally gives the 1/2? (pre-registered: PREREG_66.md) -- today's data decide
Square-root sizes come from tallies (a count of N random steps is uncertain by sqrt(N)). Three fixed shapes, no dark-energy
parameter, fitted with DESI DR2 BAO + Planck compressed CMB + each supernova set (iter66_fits_raw.txt):

| Delta chi2 vs Lambda | Pantheon+ | DES-Dovekie | Union3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| U5 SPACE tally (Claim 1): cells across the comoving horizon, rho ~ (aH)^(-1/2) | -5.4 | -7.2 | -6.8 |
| U3 LIGHT tally: cells crossed by light since the start, rho ~ eta^(1/2) | +10.8 | +13.8 | +8.0 |
| U4 CLOCK tally: physical ticks since the start, rho ~ t^(1/2) | +75.0 | +90.7 | +52.6 |

- As expected: the always-rising tallies (light, clock) are strongly disfavoured (16-21 and 60-98 in chi2 behind Claim 1).
- The data pick the SPACE tally: dark energy ~ sqrt(number of original comoving cells across today's comoving Hubble radius).
  This gives Claim 1's 1/2 a reading that does not need 'jostling layers': the size of a random tally of cells across the
  horizon. The horizon in comoving units grows while the universe decelerates and shrinks once it accelerates -- hence the
  rise, the peak at z ~ 0.68 and the crossing of w = -1.
- Not yet derived: WHY the tally is one-dimensional (across, not the volume) and why the original comoving cells are the ones
  counted. The exponent itself is still assumed 1/2 in all three; the free fit gives 0.63 +/- 0.22 (consistent). DESI 2027 tests.

# Pre-registration, iteration 66 (committed BEFORE computing): can an uncertainty / random-tally rule give Claim 1's 1/2?
Idea (it. 'uncertainty principle' ledger entry): a quantity that is only known to the Poisson spread of a tally of N steps
carries a size ~ sqrt(N) -- the square root is where 1/2 exponents come from. Which tally? Three candidates, each a FIXED
shape (no dark-energy parameter), and each a reading of 'how many steps the grid has counted':
 U5 (Claim 1) SPACE tally: original comoving cells across the comoving Hubble radius, N ~ 1/(aH)      -> rho ~ (aH)^(-1/2)
 U3 LIGHT tally: comoving cells crossed by light since the start (conformal time eta), N ~ eta          -> rho ~ eta^(1/2)
 U4 CLOCK tally: physical ticks since the start (cosmic time t), N ~ t                                  -> rho ~ t^(1/2)
(Tracking versions -- tallies of the physical Hubble volume or past 4-volume -- are already excluded, iterations 36-37.)
In the matter era U3 and U5 coincide (eta = 2/(aH)); they part company once acceleration starts: 1/(aH) then SHRINKS (dark energy
falls after z ~ 0.68, w crosses -1), while eta and t keep growing (dark energy keeps rising, w < -1 forever).
Test with TODAY's data (our fit pipeline: DESI DR2 BAO + Planck compressed CMB + each supernova set: Pantheon+, DES-Dovekie,
Union3), each shape against Lambda.
Expectations: U3 and U4 (always rising) are DISFAVOURED relative to Claim 1 because the data prefer dark energy that is easing
off today (w0 > -1); Claim 1 keeps its Delta chi2 ~ -5 to -7 vs Lambda. If U3 or U4 beat Claim 1, the 'space tally' reading
loses and the exponent's origin is the light/clock tally instead -- recorded either way.

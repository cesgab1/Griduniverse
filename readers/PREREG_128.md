# PREREG 128: could matter have been made at 16-20 GeV? -- classical tools only
Committed before code. Coalesce: 'apply this to classical physics'.
Tools: Newton's shell theorem for the expansion rate (H^2 = 8 pi G rho / 3, Milne-McCrea Newtonian cosmology) with
rho from the measured particle content (Stefan-Boltzmann counting); thermal hopping over a barrier (Arrhenius);
rate vs expansion bookkeeping (classical kinetic theory). G, hbar, c, k measured; no Planck units.
C1 Timeline: time since start when the temperature was 160, 100, 20, 16 GeV.
C2 Hopping: matter number changes by hopping a barrier E. Hops keep up with expansion while E/T < ln(T/hbar H)
   (prefactor 1; free choice, +/-10 in the log shown). Barrier from measured Higgs strength v = 246 GeV and weak
   coupling g = 0.65: E = 4 pi v B / g with B = 1.9 (theory shape factor for a 125 GeV Higgs; range 1.5-2.7).
   Report: barrier/T at 16-20 GeV and the largest barrier that would still allow hopping there.
C3 Balance: compare weak-interaction rate (~alpha_w^2 T) with expansion at 16-20 GeV; list any particle type that
   stops being produced (freezes out) between 16 and 20 GeV.
Expectations (written now): C1 ~ 1 nanosecond (vs ~0.02 ns at 100 GeV). C2 barrier ~9 TeV = ~450 T -> frozen by
hundreds of powers of ten; hopping would need a barrier ~10x lower (<~1 TeV). C3 everything in balance (rates >>
expansion by >1e10); no freeze-out at 16-20 GeV. Overall: classical reasoning blocks creation at 16-20 GeV twice,
and the block rests on the barrier height, which has never been measured.

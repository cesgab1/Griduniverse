# PREREG 130: how small can a cloud collapse? (the fragmentation floor)
Committed before data and code. Uses lesson of 129 (addendum allowed before running).
Classical calculation: a collapsing lump keeps fragmenting while it can radiate away the heat of squeezing; it stops
when it becomes opaque to its own heat. Minimum mass from measured cloud temperature (~10 K), mean molecular weight
(2.33), dust opacity (published), G, k, sigma_SB. Use a published analytic form (Rees 1976 / Low & Lynden-Bell 1976 /
Whitworth & Stamatellos 2006), reproduce its quoted value (check), then evaluate for measured conditions.
Compare: lowest-mass objects robustly found in deep JWST surveys of young clusters (IC 348, NGC 1333, Orion
Trapezium, others). Floor VIOLATED only if objects below floor/2 (mass uncertainty x2) are confirmed members in
>= 2 independent regions.
Expectation (written now): floor ~1-4 Jupiter masses; lowest robust objects ~2-5 Jupiter masses; no violation;
the 2 Jupiter-mass IC 348 object sits at the floor. Disputed claims (e.g. Orion 'JuMBOs') reported, not counted.

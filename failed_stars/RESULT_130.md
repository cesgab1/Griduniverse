# RESULT 130: how small can a cloud collapse?
Prereg: PREREG_130.md. Code: iter130_floor.py. Numbers: RESULT_130_numbers.txt.
Classical floor (heat of squeezing = heat radiated, Jeans-size lump): scales as T^(1/4) mu^(-9/4) -- matches the
published (Rees 1976) form. Our pre-registered crude version gave 0.14 Jupiter masses at 10 K vs published
3 +/- 1 -> reproduction check FAILED (factor 23). MISS logged.
Post-hoc diagnosis (not pre-registered): the floor goes as (lump-radius factor)^(-9/4); choosing the radius of a
lump in balance (virial: R = GM mu mH/5kT) gives 5.1 MJup, R = (2/5)GM mu mH/kT gives 1.1 MJup. The published
1-4 MJup sits inside this convention range. The floor is uncertain by ~x5 from the radius convention alone.
Observations (lowest robust objects): IC 348 ~2 MJup (spectroscopic members, one with a disk); NGC 1333 none below
~4-5 MJup despite sensitivity (a real deficit); NGC 2024 turnover below ~12 MJup (photometric); Upper Sco ~4 MJup.
Orion 'JuMBOs' down to 0.6 MJup: disputed -- NIRSpec follow-up found the targets checked were background objects.
Regions with confirmed objects below floor/2: 0 -> NO violation. Expectation (floor ~1-4, lowest ~2-5, IC 348 at
the floor): HIT (but our own reproduction MISSED).

LESSON 130: the observed populations stop at ~2-5 Jupiter masses, right where heat-balance says cloud collapse
must stop; failed stars are made like stars down to that floor. But the theory number is soft (x5 from one
convention); the OBSERVED cutoff is now the better measurement of the floor. Carry to 131: these objects are born
from the cloud itself, so their disks' dust is the cloud's dust -> the question becomes where the cloud's dust
came from.
